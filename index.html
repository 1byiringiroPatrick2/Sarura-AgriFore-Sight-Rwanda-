What the numbers say

In this NISR table, 25.9% of agricultural households nationally are classified as poor. The reported urban share is 27.0%, compared with 25.7% in rural areas — a difference of 1.3 percentage points.

Descriptive finding

This is a descriptive comparison only. It does not establish why poverty differs or show crop productivity.

How this supports the project

Evidence available now

  • Household poverty context for agricultural households.
  • Urban–rural descriptive comparison.
  • Baseline context for future agricultural planning analysis.

Not yet calculated

  • Crop yields by district or season.
  • Next-season production forecasts.
  • Market opportunity scores or surplus alerts.

These require actual crop production and market datasets.

Planned platform modules

1. Crop intelligenceCompare crop performance by location and season when crop observations are added.
2. Yield forecastingTrain and validate a model using historical harvest, area and input variables.
3. Market opportunity analysisCombine production trends with verified market information.
4. Early risk alertsFlag potential mismatch between expected harvest and market opportunities, with transparent evidence.

Data sources and limitations

Dataset used: “Poverty status among agricultural households in Rwanda (%)” — the supplied NISR table export.

Additional source reviewed: Rwanda Agriculture Household Survey 2024 documentation. It describes variables such as province, district, season, crops grown, seeds, harvest quantity, agricultural inputs and practices. It is survey metadata/documentation, not the underlying respondent-level dataset.

Next data needed: the actual Agriculture Household Survey / Seasonal Agricultural Survey microdata (CSV, XLSX, SAV, DTA or another authorized format), including crop, plot area, harvest quantity, season and location fields. Only then can the production and yield modules be calculated and validated.
